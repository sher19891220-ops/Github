🕊️ US-CHINA TRADE TRUCE EXTENDED TO JAN. 10 — PORT FEE THREAT SHELVED

As Xi wrapped a one-day Washington summit with Trump on Sept. 24, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed a two-month truce extension that cuts fentanyl-related tariffs and suspends a Section 301 probe into China's maritime and logistics sector — buying ocean shippers and freight importers a longer runway before the next tariff cliff.

📊 The stats/facts:
• Truce deadline pushed from Nov. 10, 2026 to Jan. 10, 2027 — a 2-month extension
• US fentanyl-related tariffs on Chinese imports cut to 10%
• China committed to buying 25 million metric tons of soybeans over 3 years, plus ~$17B in additional agricultural purchases (Bessent says China is "a little behind schedule" this year)
• Section 301 investigation into China's maritime and logistics sector — the probe behind threatened new port fees on Chinese-linked vessels — has been suspended
• China's retaliatory tariffs, non-tariff countermeasures, and rare-earth export controls also paused

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Freight-tech, ocean-carrier, and freight-forwarder marketers get a real "stability window" messaging hook — campaigns built on de-risked Q4/Q1 import planning will land now
• Brokers and 3PLs serving China-linked importers should push proactive client comms translating the tariff cut and port-fee pause into landed-cost savings, before shippers have to ask
• PR lesson: this story's broad syndication came from the summit's political drama, not the freight fine print — B2B logistics marketers can ride major macro news cycles by publishing a fast, concrete "what this means for your supply chain" translation within hours of the headline
