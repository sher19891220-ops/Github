🚨 Cargo Theft Losses DOUBLE to $304.6M — Even as Incidents Drop 26%

Verisk CargoNet's Q2 2026 report shows organized crews are stealing less often but hitting far bigger paydays, pivoting to high-value metals, tech, and business email compromise scams instead of opportunistic grabs.

📊 The stats/facts:
• 677 cargo theft incidents in Q2 2026 — down 26% YoY and 14% quarter-over-quarter
• Estimated losses hit $304.6M, up 125% YoY from $135.7M in Q2 2025
• Average theft value: $564,009
• Metal theft (mostly copper) jumped from 54 to 80 incidents
• Business email compromise remains the #1 access vector, ahead of physical theft; seafood theft rose while beverage theft fell 36 events

💡 Why marketers should care:
• "Fewer incidents ≠ less risk" is a ready-made counterintuitive hook for insurtech and freight-security brands — the data does the persuading for you
• BEC overtaking physical theft as the top attack vector signals a messaging shift: campaigns for carriers/brokers should lean into cybersecurity and identity-verification, not just GPS trackers and yard security
• The report itself is a PR playbook — a proprietary quarterly data drop from Verisk CargoNet got picked up by 6+ trade and business-wire outlets in a day; if you sit on shipment or claims data, this is the template
