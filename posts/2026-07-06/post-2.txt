🚂 Shippers Are Quietly Ditching Trucks for Rail

New AAR data shows intermodal rail volume just posted its strongest week in months — a clear signal that rising truckload rates are pushing freight off the highway and onto the rails.

📊 The stats/facts:
• Intermodal containers/trailers hit 288,739 units for the week ending June 20, 2026 — up 12.1% year-over-year
• Total U.S. rail volume (carloads + intermodal) rose 7.1% YoY to 521,998 units
• Year-to-date, combined rail traffic is up 3.1% across the first 24 weeks of 2026 (12,141,119 units)
• Metallic ores/metals (+10.3%), forest products (+9%) and grain (+8.2%) led commodity carload gains
• Coal (-6%) and motor vehicles/parts (-1.2%) were the notable laggards

💡 Why marketers should care:
• This is a live mode-shift story: as truckload spot rates run 20-25% above last year, shippers are converting freight to intermodal — a strong messaging hook for rail, intermodal and multimodal-TMS brands ("lock in savings before capacity tightens further")
• Freight brokers and 3PLs can use this data point directly in sales conversations with shippers still defaulting to truck-only RFPs, positioning rail conversion as a cost-control story, not just a service trade-off
• Content playbook worth copying: AAR publishes raw weekly data and trade press (FreightWaves) turns it into a recurring column — an easy always-on content cadence B2B logistics brands can replicate with their own operational data

🔗 Source: FreightWaves, published July 1, 2026 (data: Association of American Railroads, week ending June 20, 2026) — https://www.freightwaves.com/news/no-june-swoon-for-surging-rail-traffic
