🚨 Strait of Hormuz Traffic Grinds to a Standstill — US Trucking Feels the Diesel Squeeze

Fresh Iranian attacks on two more tankers this week have pushed Hormuz shipping toward a standstill, and the fallout is already showing up in US freight costs.

📊 The stats/facts:
• Only 30 verified tanker transits through the Strait of Hormuz between July 17-19, down from 100+ ships/day before the war began Feb. 28, 2026
• Two Dynacom-managed vessels — the supertanker Acheloos and the tanker Kavomaleas — were struck by projectiles this week; crew evacuated safely near Oman's Musandam Peninsula
• Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps claims 4 vessels with disabled transponders attempted an "unsafe route" transit on July 19
• Oil and European gas prices spiked on July 20 before partially retreating
• US diesel has run as high as $5.52/gallon in mid-July vs. under $4/gallon pre-war — Roehl Transport reports paying $4.79/gal, up from $3.47 in January

• Real-time cost pain = real-time messaging window — carriers and shippers are actively searching for fuel-surcharge automation, rate-visibility, and hedging tools right now, so timely content will outperform evergreen this week
• War-risk and cargo insurance just became a mainstream conversation instead of a niche one — insurtech and logistics marketers have a rare opening to educate buyers who'd normally scroll past "marine war risk" content
