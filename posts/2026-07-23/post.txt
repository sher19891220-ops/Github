🚢 Record-Breaking Import Surge Hits U.S. Ports Right Before the Tariff Cliff

Shippers just pushed more containers through U.S. ports in July than in any month in history — racing to beat a 10% tariff that expires at midnight tonight.

📊 The stats/facts:
• July 2026 imports are projected to hit 2.47 million TEUs — the highest monthly total ever recorded, beating the prior record of 2.4M TEUs set in May 2022
• China-origin cargo hit 814,000 TEUs in June, up 27.4% year-over-year
• The Section 122 10% global tariff surcharge expires at 12:01 a.m. ET on July 24, 2026 — a hard 150-day statutory limit only Congress can extend
• Despite record volume, Los Angeles/Long Beach saw minimal ship anchoring over the July 4 weekend — no major gridlock
• West Coast transpacific ocean rates are running ~$6,700/FEU; East Coast ~$9,000/FEU, driven by GRIs and peak-season surcharges
• A fresh, higher tariff round (10-12.5% Section 301 duties) is expected as early as August if this one lapses

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Uncertainty is the story right now, not the tariff rate itself — content that helps shippers plan for 3 scenarios (extension, replacement, lapse) earns trust that a "wait and see" competitor won't get
• This is a freight-forward-then-lull demand curve — carriers, 3PLs, and brokers should run capacity campaigns and case studies now, before the likely August pullback, not after
• A record-setting, headline-friendly stat ("busiest port month ever") is a ready-made PR/social hook — pair it with your own lane-level or port-level data if you have it
