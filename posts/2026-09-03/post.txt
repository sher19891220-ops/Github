📈 Shippers Are Paying 28% More to Move LESS Freight — August's LMI Explains Why

The August Logistics Managers' Index just landed, and it captures the strangest freight market dynamic of 2026: volumes are falling, but costs keep climbing anyway.

📊 The stats/facts:
• Transportation Prices index hit 90.0 (up 3.1 pts) — the 4th time in 5 months it's crossed the rare 90+ threshold
• Transportation Capacity stayed deeply contracted at 40.0, even after its biggest one-month improvement (+11.6 pts) in 6 months
• Shipment volumes fell 2.8% year-over-year — while shipper spending jumped 28.1% YoY over the same stretch
• Transportation Utilization spiked to 70.6 (+5.6 pts) — only the 2nd time in 5 years it's cleared 70
• Warehousing Capacity loosened to 53.5 (+7.2 pts) even as Transportation Capacity stayed pinned near record tightness — a split market by mode

💡 Why marketers should care:
• "Paying more to move less" is a messaging goldmine for freight-tech, TMS, and cost-visibility vendors — the pain point right now isn't demand, it's cost control on flat-to-declining volume, and campaigns should say that explicitly
• The transportation-vs-warehousing capacity split means broker/3PL marketers should segment outreach by mode — a shipper squeezed on trucking capacity may have newly loosened warehousing options worth cross-selling
• The LMI itself is a PR playbook: a monthly proprietary survey with consistent methodology gets picked up across FreightWaves, Logistics Management, and other trade press every single month — a repeatable model for any B2B logistics brand sitting on its own data
