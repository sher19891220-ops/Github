🚨 The Tariff Cliff Just Hit: 10% Global Surcharge Expires Today, Freight Rates Already Reacting

Section 122's 10% global tariff surcharge lapsed at 12:01 a.m. EDT today (July 24) after its 150-day statutory clock ran out — but with the replacement duties still unfinished, shippers are stuck in the messiest trade-policy window of the year.

📊 The stats/facts:
• Section 122's 10% global surcharge expired at 12:01 a.m. EDT today, exactly 150 days after taking effect Feb. 24, 2026 — the maximum duration Congress allows without an extension.
• The average effective U.S. tariff rate is projected to fall from roughly 13% to roughly 7.2% overnight, per Capital Economics estimates.
• July U.S. container imports are on pace for an all-time monthly record of 2.47M TEU, topping the prior record of 2.4M TEU set in May 2022, as importers front-loaded ahead of the deadline.
• Transpacific ocean spot rates climbed another 8% this week alone on both Asia–West Coast and Asia–East Coast lanes as the frontload rush peaked.
• USTR missed its July 20 target to finalize Section 301 replacement duties (proposed at 10-12.5% across roughly 60 countries) — meaning today's relief could be short-lived.

💡 Why marketers should care:
• High-intent search moment: shippers and importers are actively searching for "what changes today" right now — trade-compliance and freight-tech marketers have a narrow but valuable window to lead with landed-cost clarity content.
• Retarget the front-loaders: companies that just rushed imports to beat this deadline are prime targets for post-surge warehousing, capacity, and inventory-financing campaigns as volumes are forecast to slide starting in August.
• PR lesson: this story spread because it was built on a single hard number (the 13%→7.2% effective-rate shift) — dozens of trade outlets repackaged that one stat within days, showing how one concrete figure outperforms a vague trend claim for pickup.
