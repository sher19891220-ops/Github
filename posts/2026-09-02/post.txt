⛽ Diesel Just Hit a 4-Month High — And Wall Street Sees No Relief Coming

National diesel prices climbed to $5.688/gallon this week, nearly matching April's peak (the costliest since mid-2022), as the U.S.-Iran war, the Russia-Ukraine conflict, and refinery strikes squeeze global fuel supply at the same time.

📊 The stats/facts:
• National average diesel: $5.688/gal Tuesday (AAA) — highest since April's Iran-war peak, just a fraction below it
• Diesel refining margins ("crack spread") are up more than 50% since June 18 and hit a record high last month
• Crude oil trading near $90.46/barrel as Middle East and Russian refinery strikes choke supply
• Goldman Sachs more than doubled its forecast for diesel refining profits, calling diesel "the epicenter of the rally"
• Trump met with U.S. oil refiners this week pushing for more domestic diesel/gasoline output ahead of November's midterms

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Fuel volatility is now a recurring, multi-month news cycle — not a one-off spike — so freight-tech and fuel-hedging/surcharge-automation vendors have a standing, urgent pain point to anchor campaigns in rather than a single moment-in-time hook
• Small carriers and owner-operators absorb this hit hardest on spot freight — insurtech, factoring, and fuel-card marketers targeting owner-operators have a concrete cash-flow story to build messaging around right now
• The political angle (a White House meeting with refiners before midterms) is a content hook of its own — brands publishing timely, data-backed fuel commentary tied to the news cycle are getting picked up across trade press right now, a model worth copying
