⛽ Diesel Margins Just Shattered an All-Time Record

U.S. refiners' diesel crack spread — the profit margin for turning crude into diesel — blew past $100 a barrel this week for the first time ever, and trucking is about to feel it at the pump.

📊 The stats/facts:
• U.S. diesel crack spread hit an intraday all-time high of $102.20/barrel on Monday, Aug. 17, 2026
• Smashes the prior record of $89/barrel set in October 2022 during the Russia-Ukraine winter fuel crunch
• Domestic diesel supplies are at their lowest level entering late August since 1996 — even as U.S. diesel exports sit at record highs
• Cause: a "near-perfect storm" — Iran war disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz, Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries (plus a Russian diesel export ban), and attacks on Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery and Libyan facilities
• Refiners are delaying maintenance to chase record margins, raising the risk of outages that push prices even higher this winter

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Fuel-surcharge messaging is about to become a battleground — carriers who explain pricing changes to shippers clearly and proactively will keep trust; those who bury it in invoices will get chargebacks and churn
• This is a built-in wedge for freight-tech and insurtech marketers: pitch real-time cost visibility and surcharge-automation tools now, while the pain is fresh and top-of-mind for shippers and brokers
• It's also a segmentation story — large carriers protected by surcharge contracts vs. small fleets and independents exposed to margin wipeout — that gives B2B marketers a sharp audience angle for content and campaigns this quarter
