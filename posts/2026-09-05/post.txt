🚨 Mexican Truckers Blockade California Border Over Visa Crackdown

Drivers shut down the Calexico East–Mexicali commercial crossing this week to protest a wave of B-1 visa revocations tied to murky U.S. cabotage enforcement — and warned the blockade could spread to more crossings.

📊 The stats/facts:
• ~1,255 commercial trucks cross the Calexico East–Mexicali port of entry daily, carrying roughly $2 billion in trade annually
• CANACAR has confirmed at least 11 drivers in the Mexicali region lost B-1 visas, while unofficial industry estimates put revocations as high as 25,000 across Mexico's entire northern border in 2026
• Drivers say U.S. inspectors are pulling visas without clear guidance on where a legal international haul ends and prohibited domestic ("cabotage") hauling begins
• Parallel protests also broke out at the San Luis Río Colorado crossing, with organizers warning of expansion to Tijuana and beyond
• CANACAR says it hasn't had real dialogue with CBP on the issue since a February meeting

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Cross-border capacity risk just became a live, concrete story instead of a hypothetical — compliance, customs-broker, and border-visibility platforms serving carriers/3PLs/brokers have an urgent pain point to build campaigns, webinars, and sales outreach around right now
• The core ambiguity ("what actually counts as cabotage?") is a content vacuum — whoever publishes the clearest plain-language explainer this week earns search visibility and trust with cross-border shippers and carriers
• It's a case study in what makes freight news travel: a dramatic action (a border blockade), a hard dollar figure ($2B), and unresolved regulatory uncertainty got this picked up across FreightWaves, Yahoo Finance, and Transport Topics within days — a formula worth mirroring in PR outreach
