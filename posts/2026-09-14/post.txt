🚢 US IMPORT SURGE HITS RECORD HIGHS AS "PEAK SEASON" REFUSES TO END

NRF and the Port of Los Angeles both say the import boom nobody expected to last is still accelerating, with September now on track to be the single busiest cargo month of 2026.

📊 The stats/facts:
• Port of LA moved 2.9M TEUs June–August — the busiest 3-month stretch in the port's history, including 955,907 TEUs in August alone
• NRF now forecasts September 2026 imports at 2.31M TEUs, up 9.6% year-over-year and the peak month of the entire year
• August import containers ran 7% above the 5-year average, while export containers fell 9% YoY and empty containers rose 4% YoY — a lopsided trade flow
• NRF VP Jonathan Gold: "We thought the peak season would be mostly behind us by now, but that's not the case."
• Drivers stacking up: typhoon-driven delays at Chinese ports, Panama Canal drought rerouting, tariff front-loading, and Iran-conflict shipping diverted away from the Strait of Hormuz toward West Coast routes

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Extended peak season means extended urgency for anyone selling drayage capacity, warehouse space, or supply-chain visibility tools — the "just-in-case" inventory narrative still has legs into Q4 campaigns
• Retail and DTC marketers should watch inbound congestion risk when planning holiday launch dates and promo timing — a slower Port of LA turn could delay shelf-ready inventory
• Content angle: pair one hard number (2.31M TEUs, +9.6% YoY) with a clear "what it means for your supply chain" takeaway — that framing is exactly what's driving this story's spread across trade press
