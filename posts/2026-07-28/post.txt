⚖️ $604 Million Verdict Just Rewrote Freight Broker Liability

A Dallas jury hit C.H. Robinson with a $604M award over a fatal 2021 I-20 pileup — the first major test of broker liability since the Supreme Court cleared the way for these lawsuits, and Wall Street reacted instantly.

📊 The stats/facts:
• $604M in compensatory damages awarded July 23-24 in Lipe v. Lupus Superior (Dallas County)
• C.H. Robinson found 68% liable — 23% directly, plus vicariously liable for the driver's 45% as his "borrowed employer"
• C.H. Robinson's insurance: just a $135M limit against a $604M verdict, with a $10M deductible
• CHRW stock fell 9.25% ($19/share) the day of the verdict; RXO dropped 7.71%, Landstar 3.68% — a sector-wide selloff
• The case follows the May 2026 SCOTUS ruling (Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II) that stripped brokers of federal F4A liability protection for negligent carrier selection

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Freight-tech and insurtech marketers: carrier-vetting, safety-scoring, and compliance-monitoring tools just became a board-level urgency, not a nice-to-have feature — lead with risk reduction, not efficiency
• 3PL and broker marketers: "we vet every carrier" is now a trust claim buyers will actually interrogate — back it with process, not adjectives
• PR lesson: C.H. Robinson's fast, on-record response ("safety is core to how we operate," calling on Congress for clearer rules) shows how to hold a message under a stock-moving crisis — study the framing, not just the outcome
