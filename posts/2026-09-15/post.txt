🚨 CROSS-BORDER TARIFFS JUST HIT 75% — NEW U.S.-CANADA RULES TOOK EFFECT TODAY

At 12:01 a.m. ET this morning, new U.S. proclamations reshuffled the Section 338 tariff list on Canadian goods and, for the first time, let it stack on top of existing duties — and cross-border freight is already feeling the squeeze.

📊 The stats/facts:
• Effective today (Sept. 15), the U.S. added 122 HTS classifications to the 50% Section 338 duty (incl. ATVs and more dairy) while removing 8, including rock salt, cement, and fishing rods
• A new stacking rule lets the 50% Section 338 duty apply on top of the existing 25% Section 232 duty — pushing combined rates to 75% on select aluminum, steel, and motor-vehicle-linked goods
• Import bans on select Canadian alcohol, dairy, and motor vehicles take effect Sept. 29 — a further escalation beyond tariffs alone
• Canada's retaliatory tariffs (15%, 25%, and 50%) on $27.6B of U.S. exports have been in effect since Sept. 8
• Cross-border truck freight volumes are already down 8-12% YoY in Q1, per-mile rates on affected lanes are up 6-10%, and truckload costs are projected up 16-17% YoY as equipment imbalances build

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Instant messaging hook for anyone selling trade-compliance software, customs brokerage, HTS classification tools, or cross-border freight visibility platforms — "the rules just changed again" is a live search and conversation trigger today
• 3PL and broker marketers should get ahead of client questions with lane-level impact content now — the empty-truck equipment imbalance (loads south, no freight back north) is the sleeper cost story shippers haven't priced in yet
• PR lesson: this story is spreading because trade-compliance firms are publishing near-identical "what changed, what to do" alerts within hours of each other — a clear action checklist beats deep analysis when you want fast syndication on a fast-moving regulatory story
