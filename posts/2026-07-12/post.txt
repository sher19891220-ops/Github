🚢 US ports about to smash a pandemic-era import record — before the tariff clock runs out

Shippers are racing to beat a July 24 tariff deadline, and it's about to blow past the busiest month in port history.

📊 The stats/facts:
• July imports are projected to hit 2.47 million TEUs — topping the prior all-time record of 2.4 million TEUs set in May 2022
• The rush is driven by the Section 122 10% global tariff, which expires at 12:01 a.m. on July 24, 2026
• Right behind it: proposed Section 301 "forced-labor" tariffs covering 60 economies (~99% of US import value) — 10% for 14 economies including Canada, Mexico, the EU and UK, 12.5% for 46 others including China, Vietnam, India and Japan
• Earlier this year, a Supreme Court ruling invalidated the April 2025 reciprocal tariffs, triggering roughly $20 billion in refunds to importers
• Analysts expect bookings to start waning within weeks as this front-loaded wave clears the pipeline

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Urgency sells right now — carriers, brokers, and freight-tech vendors can build "beat the tariff cliff" campaigns around the July 24 deadline while it's still top of mind for shippers
• Policy whiplash is the story arc — a content/PR angle here is "how we help you navigate volatility," which resonates far more than static rate-sheet messaging in a market this jumpy
• Target ports and inland regions seeing the surge (LA/Long Beach, Savannah, Houston) — capacity-constrained shippers there are actively shopping for reliable partners right now, not just cheap ones
