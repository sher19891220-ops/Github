🚨 EPA Moves to Gut 2027 Diesel Truck Emissions Rule — Comments Close Aug. 29

The EPA is rolling back Biden-era heavy-duty diesel emissions requirements right as the public comment window closes next week, trading an estimated $12B in industry savings against up to $200B in projected public health benefits.

📊 The stats/facts:
• EPA pegs industry savings at ~$12 billion (up to $6,000 per new diesel truck) through 2045
• Emissions warranty shrinks from 10 years/450,000 miles back to 5 years/100,000 miles
• The DEF "derate" mechanism — which cuts engine power when emissions controls fail — gets replaced with a dashboard warning light instead
• Manufacturers can pay nonconformance penalties rather than hit the 35 mg/hp-hr NOx standard
• Public comment period closes August 29, 2026; the rule being weakened was projected to prevent ~2,900 premature deaths/year and deliver ~$200B in health benefits by 2045

💡 Why marketers should care:
• A hard compliance deadline (Aug. 29) is a built-in urgency hook for freight-tech, telematics, and emissions-compliance vendors — "before the rule changes" messaging has a real expiration date this week
• Fleets are weighing MY2026 purchases now vs. waiting on a cheaper, shorter-warranty MY2027 truck — a live decision moment OEM and dealer marketing can target immediately
• Shorter warranties and a "warning light instead of forced derate" shift breakdown and liability risk onto owner-operators and small fleets — a timely opening for extended-warranty and fleet-risk/insurtech products
