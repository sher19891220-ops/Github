⛽ Diesel Just Had Its Biggest Weekly Jump of the Summer — and It's Not Slowing Down

National diesel spiked 19.7¢ in a single week to $5.454/gallon, driven by drone strikes on Russian refineries and Middle East refinery damage — and freight budgets are absorbing the hit in real time.

📊 The stats/facts:
• National average diesel: $5.454/gallon, up 19.7¢ in one week — the biggest weekly move of the summer
• Diesel is up over 70% since late February 2026
• Refining margin (crack spread) between crude and diesel has stretched past $90/barrel — a record level
• US refineries are running at 97% utilization with distillate inventories below normal
• Drone strikes have pushed Russian refining capacity to 25-year lows, while damaged Middle East refineries remain offline

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Fuel volatility is now a top-3 budget conversation for shippers and carriers — content, case studies, and ROI calculators that quantify "cost per mile under $5.45 diesel" will outperform generic brand messaging right now
• This is a geopolitics-meets-logistics story — freight-tech, TMS, and route-optimization marketers have a live news hook to pitch fuel-efficiency and dynamic-routing features as direct cost offsets
• Carriers and 3PLs are actively re-pricing contracts mid-cycle — sales and PR teams should lean into "rate transparency" and "how we're protecting your margin" messaging while the pain is top of mind for buyers
