📈 Trucking Contract Rates Just Hit a Record — While Freight Volume Craters

DAT's latest data shows dry van and reefer contract rates posted their largest June-to-July gains on record, even as shipment volumes dropped across the board — a sign capacity, not demand, is now driving the market.

📊 The stats/facts:
• Dry van contract linehaul rates rose 13¢/mile from June to July — the largest June-to-July increase on record
• Reefer contract rates climbed 9¢/mile over the same span, also a record
• Dry van freight volume fell 6% month-over-month; reefer volume dropped 5% (steepest July decline in 6 years); flatbed volume sank 8%
• DAT analyst Dean Croke: "When rates rise this quickly as volumes fall, it indicates that available capacity is exerting greater influence on pricing"

💡 Why marketers should care:
• Capacity-crunch messaging sells right now — carriers, 3PLs, and brokers can pivot from "we move more freight" to "we guarantee capacity when others can't," a premium/reliability angle shippers will pay for
• DAT turned a proprietary dataset into a syndicated news hit — a playbook for freight-tech and insurtech marketers: package your data into one record-breaking headline stat and trade press picks it up for free
• Rates are decoupling from volume, so shipper-facing campaigns should lead with rate-lock and forward-contracting offers instead of "lowest rate" promises
