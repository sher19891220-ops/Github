📦 UPS Just "Fired" Its Biggest Customer — And Wall Street Is Coming Around

UPS says its 18-month plan to walk away from low-margin Amazon volume is officially complete, and it just raised full-year guidance because of it.

📊 The stats/facts:
• UPS cut ~2 million lower-quality Amazon packages per day as part of a full network reconfiguration
• Amazon now makes up just 8.8% of UPS's business, down from more than 13% previously
• The pullback banked $4.5 billion in cost savings, with another $3 billion targeted by end of 2026
• UPS raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to ~$91.2 billion, up from a prior $89.7 billion forecast
• Q2 net income still fell to $604M (down from $1.28B a year ago) as transition costs bite — even as the strategic story gets told as a win

💡 Why marketers should care:
• It's a live ICP case study: UPS chose margin over volume, deliberately walking away from its single largest account to chase healthcare, international, and small-business shippers — a model for any B2B team pressured to chase "big logo" deals that don't actually pay off
• The comms playbook is worth studying: UPS turned a revenue hit and a falling net income number into a "win" narrative by leading with forward-looking metrics (packages cut, savings banked, guidance raised) instead of burying them — trade press rewarded the clear before/after framing
• Watch the parcel battleground it opens up: as UPS retreats from cheap Amazon volume, Amazon Shipping is simultaneously courting UPS/FedEx customers with undercut pricing — carriers, brokers, and freight-tech vendors should expect "premium reliability" vs. "cheap and fast" messaging wars to intensify
